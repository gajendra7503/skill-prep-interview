Prompting skill is only half the job. The other half is judgement: what you
may put into an AI tool, what you may do with what comes out, and when not to
use AI at all. In a regulated Indian business the stakes are concrete —
client trust, regulatory action and personal accountability. This lesson
gives practical rules; your company's AI policy and compliance team always
have the final word.

## Data privacy: what never goes into an unapproved tool

Treat any AI tool your company has not approved like a public website. Do
not paste:

- **Identity numbers**: PAN, Aadhaar, passport, voter ID, bank account and
  card numbers.
- **Client data**: names with contact details, portfolios, transaction
  history, KYC documents, health or insurance information.
- **Confidential business data**: unreleased results, deal terms, pricing
  strategy, source code under NDA, credentials.

Approved enterprise tools may have contracts covering data retention and
training. Know which tools are approved for which data classes, and when in
doubt, anonymise first:

```text
Before:  Rohan Mehta (PAN ABCDE1234F, +91 98xxxxxx10) wants to redeem ₹4,50,000.
After:   Client A wants to redeem an amount in the ₹4-5 lakh range.
```

Most drafting, summarising and rewriting tasks work just as well on the
anonymised version.

## India's DPDP Act, at a high level

The **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** governs how organisations
process digital personal data of individuals in India. In broad terms it
expects a lawful basis (usually consent) for processing, use limited to the
stated purpose, reasonable security safeguards, and respect for individuals'
rights such as correction and erasure. The Act provides for significant
financial penalties for certain failures, up to ₹250 crore per instance.

Pasting a client's details into an external AI service is processing that
data and may share it with a third party. You are not expected to interpret
the law yourself — this summary is not legal advice. Follow your company's
data policy, and consult the compliance or data protection team before using
personal data in any new AI workflow. Sector regulators may add further
requirements on top.

## Bias and fairness

Models learn from human text and can reproduce its biases — around gender,
region, caste, religion, language or age. Risky uses include screening CVs,
scoring loan or credit eligibility, writing performance reviews, and
profiling customers.

- Don't let AI make or rank decisions about people without human review and
  an approved process.
- Test with counterfactual pairs: same CV, change "Priya" to "Pradeep" or
  "Lucknow" to "Bengaluru". Different outputs are a red flag.
- Remove irrelevant attributes (name, photo, age, address) before asking for
  an assessment.
- Ask whether the criteria themselves are fair, not just the model.

## Disclose AI use

Be open about where AI helped, following your team's norms. A client-facing
email drafted with AI and reviewed by you may not need a label, but a report
whose analysis was largely AI-generated should say so to its reviewers.
Never present AI output as independent research, a human expert's opinion,
or a verified fact when it is not.

## You are accountable for the output

"The AI wrote it" is not a defence. Before you send, publish or act:

- **Check facts and numbers** against source systems. Models can invent
  figures, circulars, section numbers and citations confidently.
- **Re-run calculations** yourself — returns, tax, interest, totals.
- **Read every line** of client communication as if you wrote it, because
  you did.
- **Review code** for correctness and security like any colleague's pull
  request.

## Regulated content goes through compliance

Anything resembling financial claims — returns, performance, product
comparisons, "safe" or "guaranteed" language, tax benefits — is regulated.
Regulators such as SEBI, RBI and IRDAI have rules on advertisements and
communications. AI drafts are especially prone to overclaiming:

```text
AI draft:  "This fund is a safe way to earn 15% returns every year."
Problems:  "safe" and an assured return figure; no risk disclosure.
Action:    Do not edit and send. Route through the compliance review process.
```

A useful prompt habit is to ask the model to flag risky phrasing, but treat
that as a first pass only. Compliance approval is a human step.

## When not to use AI

- The task needs data you are not allowed to share with the tool.
- The output will be used without anyone able to verify it.
- It is a decision about a person's job, credit or claim with no human
  review.
- You need guaranteed precision (legal filings, exact regulatory text) and
  can't check every word.
- The situation calls for a human relationship — bad news, a grievance, a
  bereaved client.

### Try it yourself

Pick a task you would like to hand to AI this week. Write three lines:
which data it involves and whether your approved tool may hold it, how you
will verify the output, and who must review it before it goes out. Then
anonymise one real (internal) example and test whether the anonymised
version still gives a useful result.
