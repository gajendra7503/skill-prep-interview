Writing and summarising are where most professionals first use an AI
assistant — and where small prompt changes make the biggest visible
difference. The recurring theme: tell the model **who** will read the text,
**what** it must contain, and **what it must not change**.

## Emails: give the situation, not just the topic

A weak prompt leaves the model to guess the relationship, the facts and the
desired outcome.

```text
Write an email to a customer about their delayed refund.
```

An improved prompt supplies the facts and the constraints:

```text
Draft a reply to the customer email below. I work in customer service at
Sunrise Home Appliances (a fictional company).

Facts you may use:
- Refund of ₹18,450 for order SH-20931 was approved on 3 October.
- Bank credit normally takes 5–7 working days after approval.
- Ticket reference: CS-77812.

Constraints:
- Apologise once for the delay; do not blame the bank.
- Do not promise a specific credit date — we cannot guarantee it.
- Under 150 words, plain English, sign off as "Neha Sharma, Customer Care".

<customer_email>
...
</customer_email>
```

Notice the explicit "do not promise" rule: models are helpful by default and
will happily invent a reassuring date unless told why they should not.

## Meeting-notes summaries

Raw notes or transcripts are long and repetitive. Ask for a structure that
matches how the summary will be used.

```text
Summarise the meeting transcript below for team members who missed it.

Format:
1. Decisions made (bullet list)
2. Action items as "Owner — task — due date". If no due date was stated,
   write "date not agreed" rather than guessing.
3. Open questions

Only include what was actually said. If speakers disagreed and nothing was
settled, list it under open questions.

<transcript>
...
</transcript>
```

The instruction about missing dates matters: without it, a model may fill
gaps with plausible but invented deadlines.

## Rewriting for tone and audience

The same facts need different framing for different readers. Name the
audience and what they care about.

```text
Rewrite the incident note below twice:

A) For the affected customer: empathetic, no internal jargon, explain what
   happened and what they need to do (nothing, or reset their password).
B) For my manager, Rajesh Iyer: three bullets — impact (number of users,
   duration), root cause, and next step. Neutral and factual.

Keep every number, time and name exactly as written in the original.

<incident_note>
...
</incident_note>
```

## Preserving facts while editing

When you ask for "polish" or "make it shorter", models sometimes round
numbers, merge two dates, or soften a condition ("subject to approval"
disappears). Guard against this explicitly, then verify:

```text
Edit the paragraph for clarity and grammar only. Do not change any amount,
date, name, percentage or condition. After the edited text, list every
number that appears in it so I can check them against the original.
```

Compare figures such as ₹1,25,000 or "31 March 2027" yourself before sending.
For policy documents and anything legal or regulatory, a human must review
the final wording.

## Translation, Hindi and Hinglish

Models translate between English and Hindi well, but a few practical points
help:

- **Specify the register.** "Formal Hindi suitable for a bank letter" and
  "conversational Hindi for WhatsApp" produce very different text.
- **Script matters.** Say whether you want Devanagari or romanised Hindi.
- **Hinglish input is normal.** Customers often write mixed Hindi-English in
  Roman script; ask the model to understand it but reply in the language the
  customer used, or in the language your policy requires.
- **Keep terms untranslated where needed** — product names, "EMI", "KYC",
  reference numbers.
- **Have a fluent speaker review** anything customer-facing or official.

```text
Translate the notice below into formal Hindi in Devanagari script for a
printed branch notice. Keep "KYC", "EMI" and all dates and amounts in their
original form. Flag any sentence where the meaning is ambiguous.
```

## Give a style sample

Describing a style ("crisp, warm, not salesy") is less reliable than showing
it. Paste two or three short examples of writing you like inside a tag such
as `<style_examples>` and ask the model to match the tone and length — while
making clear the examples are for style, not content to copy.

### Try it yourself

Take a real email thread from this week, remove customer names and account
details, and write a reply prompt that lists the allowed facts, one thing the
model must not promise, the reader, and a word limit. Then ask for a version
for your manager instead, and check that every number survived both
rewrites.
